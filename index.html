ATTENDANCE PARTNERSHIP

Family Support Specialist &
Kent School Services Integration

A practical operating resource for how Family Support Specialists, KSSN Coordinators, school teams, scholars and families connect around attendance.

ONE PURPOSE Remove barriers so scholars can attend, engage, and succeed.
ONE SHARED OPERATING MODEL

Remove attendance barriers together.

Start with the barrier, assign distinct ownership, coordinate support, and review whether attendance improves.

FSS

Attendance-focused family intervention

Understand family-related barriers, build the attendance plan, sustain follow-up, make warm handoffs, and monitor implementation and impact.

Scholar + Family

Pattern → Barrier → Ownership → Support → Impact

Scholar and caregiver voice stay visible throughout the process.

KSSN

Community School strategy + integrated supports

Coordinate community assets, partners, integrated supports, family/community engagement, and broader system response.

Shared work

Listen, identify barriers, coordinate family contact, prevent duplication, connect supports, follow through, and share patterns that may require a broader response.

School-owned conditions

Academic access, belonging, safety, behavior/discipline, scheduling, instructional access, and school processes remain school responsibilities even when FSS or KSSN supports the response.

Barrier is still unclear

  1. Bring attendance pattern, scholar/caregiver voice, and school evidence.
  2. Build a shared working hypothesis.
  3. Do not add a partner until the need is clear.
Required output: Leave with a barrier hypothesis and next evidence/action needed.

Both functions are clearly needed

  1. Name the FSS contribution.
  2. Name the KSSN contribution.

  1. Check implementation first.
  2. Ask whether the family could access/use the support.
  3. Adjust the intervention, ownership, or hypothesis based on evidence.

    04 · HOW WE WORK

    Coordinate at the right cadence.

    The purpose of the cadence is not to create more meetings. It is to move cases, identify patterns, and escalate what individual casework cannot solve.

    DAILY

    Do the assigned work.

    • Complete the intervention/support action.
    • Follow through with scholar/family or partner.
    • Document in the existing role-based system.
    Output: work completed or a clear reason it could not be completed.
    WEEKLY

    Unstick what is not moving.

    • Bring only cases that are unclear, stalled, or need both functions.
    • Review implementation and attendance change.
    • Clarify ownership and next move.
    Output: continue, adjust, intensify, or connect support.
    MONTHLY

    Find repeated barriers.

    • Look across cases for recurring needs.
    • Identify partner/resource gaps.
    • Surface school-controlled patterns.
    Output: building/Community School action or escalation.
    QUARTERLY

    Change or scale the system.

    • Review recurring barriers and response effectiveness.
    • Assess partnership capacity.
    • Decide what should become a broader strategy.
    Output: system change, partnership strategy, or district escalation.
    Rule of thumb: Do not use joint time to read through every case. Use joint time to resolve ambiguity, remove duplication, address stalled work, and identify patterns.
    ACTIVE PLAN REVIEW

    Use 10 / 20 / 30-day checkpoints to make a decision.

    For active Tier 2/3 plans, checkpoints should test both implementation and impact. Do not wait until Day 30 to correct a support that was never implemented or could not be accessed.

    Day 0

    Review: baseline attendance, root cause, scholar/family voice.

    Decide: What matched intervention, owner, dosage, goal, and review dates?

    Day 10

    Review: attendance since start, intervention fidelity, family contact/access.

    Decide: Is the trajectory beginning to improve? Continue or adjust immediately.

    Day 20

    Review: recent/cumulative attendance, barrier reduction, engagement.

    Decide: Is improvement meaningful and sustained? Continue, change, or intensify.

    Day 30

    Review: full response-to-intervention evidence.

    Decide: Has the intervention changed the pattern? Step down, continue, or intensify.

    ContinueImplemented as intended and showing meaningful improvement.
    AdjustBarrier match is plausible, but access, dosage, implementation, or design needs correction.
    IntensifyNeed/barrier requires greater frequency, coordination, individualization, or multidisciplinary support.
    Step downImprovement is sustained and the intensive barrier/support need has stabilized.

    Run the School Team Decision Path

    Use one current attendance concern and move it through the six decisions.

    Define + Understand

    1. Describe the attendance pattern.
    2. Gather scholar/family voice.
    3. Check academic, belonging, safety, behavior, scheduling, and school-process factors.
    Required output: Leave with a barrier hypothesis.

    Match + Implement

    1. Choose a response that directly addresses the barrier.
    2. Assign the school/FSS/KSSN/partner role.
    3. Define what implementation should look like.
    Required output: Leave with intervention + owner + implementation expectation.

    Monitor + Decide

    1. Check fidelity/access.
    2. Review attendance impact.
    3. Continue, adjust, intensify, or change the response.
    Required output: Leave with the next decision and review point.

    05 · CASE → SYSTEM

    Stop solving the same barrier one family at a time.

    Repeated barriers are system information. Use case-level learning to decide when a building, Community School, or district response is needed.

    1Repeated case signal

    The same barrier appears across multiple scholars/families.

    →
    2Pattern confirmed

    FSS family-pattern intelligence + KSSN partner intelligence + school data point to the same issue.

    →
    3Level identified

    Decide whether the response belongs at building, Community School, or district level.

    →
    4System action

    Assign an owner, action, timeline, and measure.

    Transportation

    Several families report the same route/access problem.

    Move:Escalate to school/district transportation rather than creating separate workarounds indefinitely.

    Basic Needs

    Multiple families need the same recurring support.

    Move:KSSN explores partner capacity or a scalable Community School response.

    Belonging / Peer Conflict

    Absences cluster around the same school condition.

    Move:School leads a building-level response rather than treating each absence as an individual family issue.

    Escalate when...

    • The same barrier is recurring across cases.
    • Individual supports cannot resolve the underlying condition.
    • A school-controlled issue is affecting multiple scholars.
    • A community resource gap is limiting multiple interventions.
    • A district-level policy/process decision is required.

    07 · FIELD TOOLS

    Use the conversation to understand the barrier—not just document the absence.

    These prompts are designed for real conversations with scholars, caregivers, school teams, and partners. Use what is relevant; this is not a script.

    SCHOLAR / CAREGIVER CONVERSATION

    Questions that help uncover the barrier

    Start broad“What has been making it harder to get to school lately?”
    Look for patterns“Are there certain days, classes, times, or situations when attendance is harder?”
    Identify what helps“What is different on the days getting to school goes well?”
    Prioritize“If we could make one thing easier right now, what would help the most?”
    Check fit“Would this support actually be useful for your family? What could make it hard to use?”
    SCHOOL TEAM CONVERSATION

    Questions that prevent premature referral

    PatternWhat do the absences actually look like?
    School factorsWhat academic, belonging, safety, behavior, scheduling, or process conditions could be contributing?
    EvidenceWhat do we know from scholar/caregiver voice versus what are we assuming?
    InterventionWhat are we doing that directly addresses the barrier?
    OwnershipWhat remains the school's responsibility even if FSS or KSSN becomes involved?
    WARM HANDOFF STANDARD

    A referral is not complete when the name is sent.

    1Explain

    Tell the family why this partner/resource may help and what to expect.

    2Connect

    Whenever possible, connect the family directly to a person—not just a website or phone number.

    3Confirm

    Verify the family was able to reach the resource and access the support.

    4Follow through

    Keep the attendance goal visible and review whether the barrier changed.

    EXAMPLE

    Transportation barrier

    Weak handoff: “Here is a transportation resource. Call them.”

    Stronger handoff: “We believe transportation is contributing to the absences. I’m connecting you with ___, who can help us explore options. I’ll check back by ___ to make sure the connection happened and to see whether getting to school became easier.”

    MINIMUM DOCUMENTATION

    Capture what matters for the next decision.

    Continue using existing FSS/KSSN/school systems. Do not create another log.

    Barrier / hypothesisWhat is preventing or making attendance harder?
    InterventionWhat support is intentionally addressing that barrier?
    OwnerWho is responsible for each next step?
    ImplementationDid the support actually occur?
    Review pointWhen will the team look again?
    ImpactIs the barrier changing and is attendance improving?

    Watch for these common breakdowns

    Multiple staff contacting the family without coordinating first. A call, letter, or referral being treated as the intervention. Sending a case to FSS/KSSN before checking school-controlled barriers. No one confirming whether the family accessed the referred support. Repeating the same response even though attendance is not improving. Solving the same recurring barrier one family at a time.

    09 · TEAM TOOLS

    Keep joint time focused on decisions.

    Use these structures in attendance-team, FSS/KSSN, MTSS, or Community School conversations. They are deliberately short.

    10-MINUTE CASE HUDDLE

    For one stalled or complex case

    1. 1 min — Pattern: What is the attendance pattern?
    2. 2 min — Barrier: What do we know vs. what are we assuming?
    3. 2 min — Intervention: What is intentionally addressing the barrier?
    4. 2 min — Ownership: FSS, KSSN, School, Shared—who does what?
    5. 2 min — Impact: What evidence will tell us it is working?
    6. 1 min — Decision: Continue, adjust, intensify, connect, or escalate.
    Required output: one next move, one owner, one review point.
    20-MINUTE MONTHLY PATTERN REVIEW

    For recurring barriers

    1. 5 min — Scan: What barriers are repeating?
    2. 5 min — Interpret: What are families, partners, and school data telling us?
    3. 5 min — Level: Is this case-level, building-level, Community School, or district-level?
    4. 5 min — Act: What system response, partner strategy, or escalation is needed?
    Required output: system action + owner + timeline + measure.

    10 · QUICK REFERENCE

    Use the same language across teams.

    Action

    Something staff did—such as a call, text, letter, meeting, or referral. An action is not automatically an intervention.

    Intervention

    An intentionally selected support designed to reduce the identified attendance barrier, change behavior, or address a root cause.

    Barrier

    A condition making regular attendance harder. It may be family-, school-, community-, or system-related, or a combination.

    Warm handoff

    A supported connection to another person/resource that includes explanation, connection, confirmation, and follow-through.

    School-controlled barrier

    A condition the school can directly change, such as academic access, belonging, safety, behavior, scheduling, or school process.

    Working hypothesis

    The team's current best explanation of the barrier, based on available evidence and scholar/caregiver voice. It can change as new evidence emerges.

    Implementation

    Whether the intervention actually occurred as intended, including frequency, intensity, and follow-through.

    Impact

    Whether the barrier is changing and attendance is improving—not simply whether staff completed the action.

    IMPLEMENTATION GUARDRAILS

    Use these rules when the situation is not clear.

    Barrier before roleDo not decide “FSS or KSSN?” before understanding what is preventing attendance.
    Intervention before activityA call, letter, meeting, or referral may support the work, but it is not automatically the intervention.
    School ownership stays visibleDo not transfer school-controlled barriers outward because another role is supporting the family.
    Warm handoffs close the loopConfirm access and whether the support actually reduced the barrier.
    Implementation before escalationCheck whether the intended intervention occurred before concluding that it failed.
    Patterns trigger system thinkingWhen the same barrier repeats across cases, move beyond one-family-at-a-time work.

    INTERVENTION QUALITY CHECK

    Do not approve an intervention until these six questions are answered.

    Attendance percentage identifies the level of concern. Root-cause analysis determines the intervention. The intervention should be specific enough that the team can tell whether it actually happened and whether it changed the attendance barrier.

    1. BarrierWhat specific root cause or working hypothesis is this intervention designed to reduce?
    2. InterventionWhat support will actually change the barrier? A call, letter, referral, or resource search alone is an action.
    3. OwnerWho is accountable for implementation? Name one lead and clarify supporting roles.
    4. Dosage / frequencyHow often, how long, or under what conditions will the intervention occur?
    5. Measurable goalWhat change in the barrier and attendance pattern should the team expect to see?
    6. Review pointWhen will the team review implementation, access, scholar/family voice, barrier reduction, and attendance response?
    Document in the existing system—not a new tracker.

    FSS WORKSPACE

    Attendance-focused family intervention

    Use this workspace when the primary work is understanding a family-related attendance barrier, building a concrete attendance plan with the scholar/caregiver, following through over time, and checking whether attendance changes.

    FSS test If the work could be described only as “I contacted the family,” it is not deep enough yet. The FSS contribution should show how the barrier is being reduced and how progress will be reviewed.

    Use-now tools

    Open the tool you need. These are discussion/planning supports—not a new tracker.

    1. Barrier Conversation Guide

    Use with a scholar/caregiver when the reason for absence is unclear or based mostly on assumptions.

    1. What has been making it harder to get to school?
    2. When is attendance hardest? What is different on days it goes well?
    3. What has the family already tried?
    4. What school factors may be making attendance harder?
    5. If one thing became easier this week, what would help most?
    Leave with: a working barrier hypothesis—not just a list of concerns.
    2. Attendance Plan Builder

    Use after the barrier is reasonably clear.

    1. Barrier: What specifically is interfering with attendance?
    2. Change: What needs to be different for attendance to improve?
    3. Intervention: What support directly addresses that barrier?
    4. Owner: Who will do each part?
    5. Review: When will implementation and attendance be reviewed?
    Leave with: one matched intervention, clear ownership, and a review point.
    3. Morning Routine Problem-Solver

    Use when routines, work schedules, siblings, transportation, or morning stress are contributing.

    • Work backward from school start time.
    • Identify the first point where the routine breaks down.
    • Reduce decisions/tasks that can happen the night before.
    • Name a backup plan for the most likely disruption.
    • Check whether school-controlled flexibility/support could reduce friction.
    Leave with: a realistic routine the family believes it can use.
    4. Home Visit / Caregiver Meeting Prep

    Use to keep the meeting focused on understanding and problem-solving rather than compliance alone.

    • Bring the attendance pattern—not assumptions.
    • Know what prior actions/supports have already occurred.
    • Ask what the family wants the school to understand.
    • Identify barriers the school can address directly.
    • End with specific next steps and a follow-up date.
    Leave with: shared understanding + next actions, not simply “meeting completed.”
    5. Follow-Up / Impact Review

    Use after an intervention has had enough time to occur.

    1. Did the intervention occur as intended?
    2. Was the family able to use/access the support?
    3. Is the identified barrier becoming easier?
    4. What changed in attendance pattern?
    5. Continue, adjust, intensify, add support, or change the hypothesis?
    Leave with: a decision—not “continue monitoring” by default.
    6. Warm Handoff Prep

    Use before connecting the family to KSSN, Behavioral Health, DHHS, health care, or another partner.

    • Explain why the resource matches the barrier.
    • Clarify what the family can expect.
    • Connect directly to a person when possible.
    • Confirm access after the connection.
    • Keep the attendance goal visible after the partner joins.
    Leave with: a closed-loop connection, not a referral sent into the system.

    KSSN adds more than a referral.

    The Community School Coordinator integrates, aligns, and coordinates the community school strategy with the principal, school team, families, scholars, and partners. Use KSSN when the attendance barrier requires community-school infrastructure—not simply another person making the same family contact.

    1. Integrated supports

    Coordinate school and community resources around identified needs.

    • Connect physical, behavioral-health, social-service, youth-development, and other supports.
    • Coordinate referrals and reduce fragmented service delivery.
    • Bring relevant attendance trends into support planning.
    • Confirm that partner supports align with the school’s broader plan.

    2. Family + community engagement

    Center family and scholar voice in decisions about needs, assets, programs, and partnerships.

    • Identify what families say would improve access and engagement.
    • Build opportunities for family participation and leadership.
    • Strengthen the school as a community hub.
    • Use community wisdom alongside school data.

    3. Collaborative leadership

    Work with the principal and school leadership structures rather than operating as a separate service.

    • Integrate partners into attendance, MTSS, safety, instruction, and leadership structures where appropriate.
    • Co-lead the Community School Leadership Team.
    • Clarify partner roles, expectations, outcomes, and accountability.
    • Reduce overlapping or disconnected initiatives.

    4. Needs, assets + system response

    Use data and community knowledge to identify repeated needs and build sustainable responses.

    • Conduct ongoing needs/resource assessment.
    • Identify gaps in existing school and community capacity.
    • Develop or strengthen partnerships based on demonstrated need.
    • Move recurring barriers from individual case work to broader school/community response.

    KSSN attendance decision test

    Bring KSSN in when: the barrier requires community assets, integrated supports, partner coordination, family/community engagement, expanded opportunities, needs/assets analysis, or a broader system response.

    Do not add KSSN simply because: a scholar is absent, a family needs another phone call, or a referral needs to be sent. Those facts alone do not define the Community School Coordinator function.

    For an individual caseName the distinct KSSN contribution and how it complements—not duplicates—the FSS and school response.
    For a repeated barrierUse attendance data plus family/community voice to identify the need, existing assets, capacity gap, and possible broader response.
    For a partnerDefine the need, expected contribution, access pathway, school-team connection, outcome, and how effectiveness will be reviewed.

    KSSN test If the work could be described only as “I referred the family to a resource,” it is too narrow. KSSN should add coordination, integration, partner capacity, family/community voice, or system-level value.

    Use-now tools

    These supports reflect the Coordinator's broader Community School function.

    1. Needs + Assets Quick Scan

    Use when a barrier is appearing repeatedly or a school need is emerging.

    1. What need/barrier is showing up?
    2. Who is affected and how often?
    3. What school/community assets already exist?
    4. Where is there a capacity, access, or coordination gap?
    5. What data and family/scholar voice support the conclusion?
    Leave with: a defined need + existing assets + the gap that remains.
    2. Partner Fit Check

    Use before bringing in or expanding a partner.

    • Which identified need does the partner address?
    • What specifically will the partner contribute?
    • What school/FSS/KSSN work already exists?
    • How will duplication be prevented?
    • What outcome will indicate the partnership is helping?
    Leave with: a partner role tied to a defined need and outcome.
    3. Integrated Support Plan

    Use when multiple services are involved with the same scholar/family or barrier.

    1. Define the shared barrier/outcome.
    2. List each support already involved.
    3. Clarify what each party uniquely owns.
    4. Identify gaps, overlap, or conflicting family messages.
    5. Agree on communication and follow-through.
    Leave with: one coordinated experience for the family.
    4. Community School Leadership Team Prep

    Use to prepare a data-driven partner/leadership conversation.

    • What school data or recurring barriers need attention?
    • What do families/scholars say about the issue?
    • Which existing partners/assets can contribute?
    • Where is new partner capacity needed?
    • What decision/action should the team make today?
    Leave with: an action, owner, timeline, and measure—not partner updates only.
    5. Expanded Opportunity Match

    Use when enrichment, mentoring, after-school, youth development, or family programming may strengthen engagement.

    • What barrier/outcome is the opportunity intended to address?
    • Who is the intended group?
    • What access barriers could prevent participation?
    • How does the opportunity connect to school goals?
    • How will participation/impact be reviewed?
    Leave with: a targeted opportunity—not programming for programming's sake.
    6. Case → System Escalation

    Use when the same barrier appears across multiple cases.

    1. Confirm the repeated pattern.
    2. Estimate who/how many are affected.
    3. Identify whether the condition is school, community, or district controlled.
    4. Map partner/system options.
    5. Bring a concrete recommendation to the appropriate decision group.
    Leave with: a system response with an accountable owner.
